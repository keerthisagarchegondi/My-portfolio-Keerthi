1 · The problem

One alert can be scored at two very different scales.

Think smoke alarm, not model magic.

If a smoke alarm rings once during a long emergency, a point-by-point score credits that one instant. An event-aware rule may credit the whole event after any hit. Both views can be useful—but they answer different questions.

The audit question

What happens when exactly the same randomly generated alerts are evaluated by these two rules? This project audits the ruler used to judge alerts; it does not build or benchmark a detector.

2 · See the mechanism

Same alerts. Only the evaluation changes.

Raw scoring

Credit is assigned at each timestamp. One random hit within a long event leaves most event timestamps uncredited.

Point adjustment

After a hit lands within an event, the scoring convention gives credit across that event. The alert sequence itself is unchanged.

3 · The experiment

A controlled, paired finite-sample study.

Each condition uses the same simulated alert draws for raw and adjusted scoring, separating the effect of the evaluation convention from a change in alerts.

5 · Event-length insight

Longer events make a random hit more likely.

At q = 1%, the probability of at least one random alert in an event rises from approximately 1.0% when L = 1 to 86.6% when L = 200. Adjusted F1 rises strongly with event length even though the predictions remain random and unchanged.

6 · Analytical validation

Simulation behavior matches the event-hit probability.

P(hit event) = 1 − (1 − q)L

The analytical probability quantifies the chance that random alerts hit an event at least once. The simulation results align with that mechanism across the tested conditions.

7 · What it means

I didn’t build another anomaly detector. I audited the ruler used to judge one.

Why it matters

Evaluation design, measurement validity, and experimental controls can materially affect how results are interpreted. The work demonstrates paired experimental design, analytical validation, and reproducible reporting.

What to ask next

Before comparing detector results, make the scoring convention explicit, test its behavior under relevant event structures, and report limits alongside summary metrics.
